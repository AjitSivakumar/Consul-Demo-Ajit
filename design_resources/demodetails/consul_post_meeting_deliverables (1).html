Question surfaced during meeting
Why does Preet need both the Iridium Extreme and Iridium GO! — aren't two devices redundant?
Consul's answer (full form)
The Extreme and GO! address entirely separate capability gaps and cannot substitute for one another. The Extreme is a standalone handset handling voice calls, SBD messaging, and GPS tracking. The GO! is a satellite hotspot enabling smartphone-based photo uploads and richer data — but requires a paired device to operate and cannot make voice calls independently. For Preet's specific requirements (daily voice blogs + photo uploads to social + real-time location tracking), neither device alone is sufficient. Critically, the GO! is configured once pre-departure and runs passively in the background for the entire expedition — Preet physically interacts with only the Extreme on the ice. Two devices in the kit; one device in hand.

How does the GO! connect to Preet's phone in a polar environment — does Bluetooth or Wi-Fi degrade at extreme temperatures?
The GO! creates a local Wi-Fi hotspot (not Bluetooth). Wi-Fi signal integrity is unaffected by cold; the limiting factor is device battery performance, not signal protocol. The GO! is rated to −20°C operating temperature and is typically stored in an insulated carry case between transmissions.
Internal product spec sheet (GO! environmental ratings)
T2
What's the latency on the GO! data connection — can it realistically support photo uploads of expedition-quality images?
The GO! delivers 2.4 kbps data over the Iridium network. Compressed social-media-ready images (JPEG, ~500KB–1MB) typically transfer in 30–90 seconds. RAW or uncompressed files are not suitable. For Preet's social media use case, this is operationally sufficient.
Internal product architecture documentation (GO! data throughput specs)
T3
If the GO! fails mid-expedition, does Preet still have full comms or does her setup degrade significantly?
The Extreme operates entirely independently — voice calls, SBD messaging, and GPS tracking continue uninterrupted. The only capability lost is photo upload. This is the core argument for dual-device: the Extreme is the survival comms layer; the GO! is an additive media layer.
