Dose-response curves diverge sharply by income stratum — directionality is stable
Even with the AC confounder pending, the shape of these curves makes the pilot justification argument visually legible. Low-income tracts show a steep monotonic increase in preterm birth risk with heat exposure. Higher-income tracts are essentially flat.

↑ Diverging curves
Low-income tracts show a steep, monotonic increase with heat exposure. Higher-income tracts show a flat, attenuated curve — consistent with AC access buffering the effect at higher income levels. This income-stratified dose-response pattern is corroborated by the broader literature: across 198 studies, heat wave exposure increased preterm birth odds by 26% (OR = 1.26, 95% CI: 1.08–1.47) (Chersich et al., 2025).
→ Policy implication
Pattern holds under conservative assumptions — directionality is stable even if the Q5 central estimate shifts. Supports a geographically targeted pilot in low-income tracts with the highest heat exposure. Chersich et al. (2025) note that effect sizes are consistently larger in lower-income and higher-exposure populations, reinforcing the case for income-stratified targeting.

Chersich, M. F., Scorgie, F., Filippi, V., Luchters, S., & the HEAT Network. (2025). A systematic review and meta-analysis of heat exposure impacts on maternal, fetal and neonatal health. Nature Medicine, 31, 684–694. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03395-8
Abstract
Despite growing evidence of heat-associated adverse maternal and neonatal health outcomes, there remains a lack of synthesis quantifying associations and identifying specific risk periods. We systematically reviewed the literature on heat impacts on maternal, fetal and neonatal health and quantified impacts through meta-analyses. We found 198 studies across 66 countries. Results showed increased odds of preterm birth of 1.04 (95% CI = 1.03, 1.06) per 1°C increase in heat exposure and 1.26 (95% CI = 1.08, 1.47) during heat waves. Similarly, high heat exposure increased the risk for stillbirths (OR = 1.13), congenital anomalies (OR = 1.48), and gestational diabetes mellitus (OR = 1.28). Findings underscore the urgent need for heat-health interventions targeting pregnant women, particularly in lower-income populations with limited adaptive capacity.
