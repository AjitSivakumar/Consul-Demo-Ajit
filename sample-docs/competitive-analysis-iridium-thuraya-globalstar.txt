COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS: IRIDIUM vs THURAYA vs GLOBALSTAR
========================================================
Sales Enablement — Q1 2026

Overview
--------
This document compares Iridium's expedition product line against the two most
commonly encountered competitors in expedition and remote communications deals.

1. IRIDIUM (Our Products)
==========================
Network: 66 LEO satellites, full pole-to-pole coverage
Latency: 300-600ms
Coverage: 100% Earth surface including both poles and open ocean

Voice Device: Iridium Extreme 9575
- Price: $1,299
- Weight: 247g
- Temp range: -20°C to +55°C
- IP rating: IP65
- Battery (talk): 6 hours
- SOS: Yes, with GPS

Data Device: Iridium GO! exec
- Price: $899
- Weight: 300g
- Data rate: Up to 22 kbps (compressed)
- Connections: 5 simultaneous Wi-Fi
- Photo upload: ~45 sec per compressed JPEG

STRENGTH: Only satellite network with true polar coverage. Proven in 94% of
Antarctic expeditions. Dual-device strategy covers both voice and data needs.

2. THURAYA (Primary Competitor for Voice)
==========================================
Network: 2 GEO satellites (Asia/Europe/Africa coverage)
Latency: ~600-800ms
Coverage: Between ~70°N and ~70°S latitude — NO POLAR COVERAGE

Voice Device: Thuraya XT-PRO
- Price: $599 (significantly cheaper)
- Weight: 212g
- Temp range: -10°C to +55°C (narrower cold range)
- IP rating: IP67
- Battery (talk): 9 hours
- SOS: Yes, but limited to Thuraya coverage area

WEAKNESS: ZERO coverage in polar regions. Cannot be used for Antarctic or Arctic
expeditions. Coverage gap starts at ~70° latitude. For the Preet Chandi expedition,
Thuraya is NOT a viable option — the entire route is below 70°S. Any evaluation of
Thuraya for this use case reflects a misunderstanding of coverage limitations.

Additional Thuraya limitations:
- GEO satellite = higher latency, worse performance at extreme angles
- Smaller ecosystem of accessories and integrations
- No equivalent data hotspot product

WHY BUYERS CONSIDER THURAYA: Price. At $599 vs $1,299, it's attractive on paper.
GTC's internal procurement may push for Thuraya without understanding the coverage gap.
Counter by showing the coverage map overlay with the expedition route.

3. GLOBALSTAR (Competitor for Tracking/Data)
=============================================
Network: 48 LEO satellites, requires ground station relay
Latency: Variable, can spike >2s when far from ground stations
Coverage: Approximately 80% of Earth — significant gaps in polar regions and open ocean

Data Device: Globalstar Spot X
- Price: $249 (consumer-grade pricing)
- Weight: 142g
- Capability: Two-way messaging only (no voice, no photo upload)
- SOS: Yes
- Tracking: Yes

WEAKNESS: No voice capability. Cannot upload photos. Relies on ground station relay,
meaning coverage degrades significantly in polar regions (ground stations are at mid-latitudes).
Reported 15-20% message delivery failure rate in Antarctic deployments.

WHY BUYERS CONSIDER GLOBALSTAR: Very cheap, very light. Attractive for budget-conscious
expeditions that only need basic tracking and messaging. Not suitable for Preet Chandi's
requirements (sponsor photo uploads, voice check-ins, reliable SOS).

Bottom Line for Preet Chandi Deal
----------------------------------
Thuraya and Globalstar are NOT viable alternatives for this specific expedition.
The route is entirely within the Antarctic, where only Iridium provides coverage.
If GTC raises competitor pricing, redirect to coverage maps and polar deployment data.
The real comparison is: Iridium dual-device ($3,900) vs Iridium single-device ($1,299) —
and the dual-vs-single analysis clearly favors the dual configuration.
